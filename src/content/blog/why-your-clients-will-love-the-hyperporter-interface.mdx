The travel client experience has a strange shape. Someone spends five thousand euros and then receives, as the artefact of that purchase, an email with four PDFs attached. Later there will be more emails, each with a slightly different PDF, and none of them will say which is current.

They will ask you. That is the interface.

## What a client is actually asking

Almost every message a traveller sends between booking and departure is one of four questions.

- **Is it confirmed?** Not accepted — held, by the supplier, in writing.
- **What did I pay, and what is left?** With a date on the balance.
- **What happens on day four?** Times, pickups, who is meeting them.
- **Who do I contact when something moves?** At 3am, in another time zone.

None of these is a hard question. All of them are hard to answer from an inbox, which is why they arrive as messages to you rather than answers they found themselves.

## The link, not the app

A Hyperporter client gets a link. No password, no download, no account to create — a magic link that opens their trip in a browser.

On it: the itinerary as it stands right now, what is confirmed and what is still being held, the payment schedule with what has been received, and the documents they need. When day four changes, the page changes. There is no v3 to send, because there is no version — there is the trip.

## Why that changes the relationship

Two things happen when a traveller can see the trip.

The first is obvious: they stop asking. The messages that used to arrive on a Sunday evening are answered before they are written, because the answer was already on the page.

The second matters more. A client who can see what was confirmed, what it cost and what is coming next reads the whole thing as organised — and reads *you* as organised. The itinerary was always good. Now the delivery of it matches.

## It is your brand, not ours

The page carries your name, your colours and your wording. On Showcase there is a small "Powered by Hyperporter"; from Autopilot up it is gone entirely. Your client does not learn a new company's name at the moment they are most pleased with you.

## What it does not do

It does not replace you. A page cannot reassure someone whose flight has just been cancelled, and it does not try to. What it does is take the four questions that never needed a person off your desk, so the ones that do need a person get you at your best — awake, with the whole trip in front of you, rather than at 3am reconstructing it from a thread.

The clients notice that. They rarely say "your portal is good." They say you were easy to work with.
